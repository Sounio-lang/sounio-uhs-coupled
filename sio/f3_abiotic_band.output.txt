== channel (b): abiotic mineral redox -- NOW BOUNDED ==
   Truche et al. 2010, GCA 74:2894-2914, run #P20. A measured null:
   90 C, 8 bar H2, calcite-buffered, 40-80 um pyrite, sampled to
   2472 h, with aqueous sulfide below 0.1 mmol/L at EVERY timepoint.
   Pyrite reduction consumes at most one H2 per sulfide, so that
   sulfide ceiling is an H2 ceiling.
   H2 consumed over 2472 h, as a fraction of the field inventory  3311696913498e-16
   scaled to 285 d with their own exponent t^0.47               5343110697385e-16
   So channel (b) is under about 0.05 % of the stored hydrogen.

   NO TEMPERATURE EXTRAPOLATION IS USED. The bound is measured at
   90 C and applied to a 40 C field; since the activation energy is
   positive the reaction is SLOWER at 40 C, so the 90 C figure is
   conservative. Only the SIGN of the temperature dependence is
   relied on, never its magnitude -- and that matters, because
   Truche's fitted rate constants stop at 120 C, so a quantitative
   extrapolation to 40 C would run 80 C below the calibration floor
   on a parabolic law whose mechanism the authors call mixed.
   CORRECTIONS.md C16 records what happened when someone did that.

== channels (c) and (d): still REFUSED, but for a sharper reason ==
   (c) abiotic methanation. There are now several MEASURED NULLS at
       UHS-like conditions -- Etiope & Ionescu 2015 report that Fe,
       Ni and Cr oxides produce NO methane below 100 C; Al-Yaseri
       2023 saw no CH4 from H2/brine/limestone at 75 C, 96.5 bar,
       125 d; Hassanpouryouzband 2022 detected only water vapour to
       ppb across 250-plus paired H2/N2 runs at 59-80 C, 1-20 MPa.
       But NONE publishes a detection limit or a rate, so none can
       be converted into a number. They bound the channel only to
       'below whatever these methods resolved', and that resolution
       is never stated.
   (d) caprock diffusion and capillary trapping. Still no transport
       data. Note the field's own caprock permeabilities are nearly
       equal for H2 and CH4 (2.3-2.7 vs 2.1-2.5 e-18 m2), which
       argues against preferential H2 diffusion but is not a flux.

   THIS MODULE STILL REFUSES TO EMIT A TOTAL ABIOTIC BAND, because
   (c) and (d) remain unbounded. A null without a detection limit
   is not a zero, and summing as though it were would assert an
   unmeasured null -- the same error as inventing a number, with
   the sign reversed.

== F3 verdict, CORRECTED -- see sio/field_mass_balance.sio ==
   The comparison this module originally made, under 2 % against
   roughly 16 % observed, was WRONG. Most of the 15.6 % not
   recovered was never consumed: about half migrated into cushion
   gas and remains in the reservoir. Decomposing the field balance
   with the paper's own numbers gives physical routes 11.0 % of
   injected H2 and biotic about 3.3 %, a ratio near 3.4 to 1.

   So the abiotic channels explain roughly 70 % of the shortfall,
   not the small fraction implied above. F3 still does not fire,
   because they do not explain all of it and the residual is
   corroborated by the CO2 balance and by measured methanogen
   growth -- but the margin is narrow, not large.

   The site's sterile control points the same way: killing the
   microbes removes 5 to 10 times the total-pressure loss.

   F3 DOES NOT FIRE. The abiotic band does not encompass the field
   point, so the coupling is NOT superfluous on this evidence.
   This is a NEGATIVE result for F3 and therefore a positive one
   for keeping the biotic model -- stated as such, since F3 was
   pre-registered as a chance to discard the whole apparatus.

   CAVEAT, stated rather than buried: dissolution is treated here
   as unrecovered. On drawdown some dissolved H2 exsolves and IS
   recovered, so channel (a) as computed is an UPPER bound on the
   dissolution loss. That makes the F3 verdict STRONGER, not
   weaker: the true abiotic dissolution loss is smaller still.
